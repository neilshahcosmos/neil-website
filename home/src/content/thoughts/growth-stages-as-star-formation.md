Stars form inside cold, diffuse clouds of gas and dust. A region of the cloud that becomes
dense enough starts to collapse under its own gravity. The collapsing core heats up and becomes
a **protostar**, still pulling in material from the surrounding cloud. Only once the core is hot
and dense enough to fuse hydrogen does it settle into a stable, long-lived **star**.

That's a good picture of how an idea develops:

1. **Nebula.** Diffuse material: a question, a few quotes, a half-remembered argument. Most
   of it will never collapse into anything.
2. **Protostar.** Something has started to pull itself together. There's a shape and a centre of
   gravity, but it's still accreting and still changing.
3. **Star.** It has ignited. The idea is stable enough to stand on its own and light up other
   things around it.

Every page in the garden carries one of these labels (see [[welcome-to-the-observatory]]).
